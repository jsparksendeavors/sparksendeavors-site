The story

What does it cost to build something? And what should it never cost you?

Building a company is one thing. Learning who you no longer need to prove wrong is another.

Josh Sparks was nine when his mother went to prison. From foster care and seventeen schools, he went on to build one of America's largest home improvement companies.

But the old voices came with him into negotiations, boardrooms, and hard conversations. The critics and ghosts who were never actually in the room still seemed to have a vote.

This memoir looks beyond the business story: at resilience and ambition, at the pursuit of approval, and at the freedom of no longer asking someone else's opinion to define a life.

Beyond the scoreboard

The author

Josh Sparks

Josh started at eighteen with a nail gun and a friend's offer of work on a Milwaukee roof. The company he went on to found became Infinity Home Services, a family of local home-exterior brands built around a mission to save communities from unscrupulous contractors.

Today he serves as founder and chairman, invests in the trades, writes, and speaks. His work brings together Stoic wisdom and entrepreneurial drive: resilience in chaos, integrity in success.

He lives in Franklin, Tennessee with his wife MaryJean and their five children.
